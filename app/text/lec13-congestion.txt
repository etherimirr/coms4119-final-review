QUIZ: UDP vs TCP

A server handling 10 clients simultaneously. How many
socket connections are maintained by the server if the
server and client use UDP?

And how many socket connections maintained by the
server if they use TCP?
Answer: 1 socket connection when UDP is used, and 10 + 1 = 11
socket connections when TCP is used.

                                                                                  Transport Layer: 3-117
What does TCP do for reliable data transfer?
§ GBN + selective repeat + a few differences
  • Sequence number, sliding window at TX and RX

  • RX sends cumulative ACKs (GBN)

  • TX maintains a single retransmission timer

  • RX keeps out-of-order packets (like SR)

  • Use fast retransmit: use duplicate ACKs to trigger early retransmission

  • Estimate timeout: retransmit if no ACK is received after timeout

  network to handle”
§ manifestations:
   • long delays (queueing in router buffers)
   • packet loss (buffer overflow at routers)

Why Does Congestion Happen?
§ Statistical multiplexing in packet switching

§ If too many packets arrive at a router in a sudden
  • The router cannot keep up with the traffic
  • Delay traffic, and the buffer may eventually overflow

                                                                         Transport Layer: 3-137
All These Windows…
§ Congestion Window: CWND
  • # of bytes sent without overflowing routers
  • Computed by the sender using congestion control algorithm


§ Flow control window: AdvertisedWindow (RWND)
  • # of bytes sent without overflowing receiver’s buffers
  • Determined by the receiver and reported to the sender


§ TCP window = min{CWND, RWND}
     • Assume for this lecture that RWND >> CWND

                                                                    Transport Layer: 3-141
How to adapt congestion window size?
§ Pay more penalty for over-sized window than under-sized window
  • Over-sized window: packet loss and retransmissions
  • Under-sized window: lower throughput


§ Additive increase, multiplicative decrease (AIMD)
  • Packet success: increase the congestion window linearly
  • Packet loss: divide congestion window by half

TCP AIMD: more
Multiplicative decrease detail: sending rate is
§ Cut in half on loss detected by triple duplicate ACK (TCP Reno)
§ Cut to 1 MSS (maximum segment size) when loss detected by
  timeout (TCP Tahoe)

Why AIMD?
§ AIMD – a distributed, asynchronous algorithm – has been
  shown to:
  • optimize congested flow rates network wide!
  • have desirable stability properties
